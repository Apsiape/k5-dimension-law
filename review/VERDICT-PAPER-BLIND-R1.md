## F1 — BLOCKING. §2.1 defines $\Sigma_n$ as the *finite-dimensionally realisable* set and then says it contains a real interval. That is a flat self-contradiction, and the definition is wrong.

**Quote (§2.1, first sentence):**
> "For $n\ge2$ let $\Sigma_n \subseteq [0,n]$ be the set of $\alpha$ for which $\alpha\cdot I$ is a sum of $n$ orthogonal projections in some matrix algebra; equivalently, the set of $\alpha$ for which the universal $C^\ast$-algebra $P_{n,\alpha}$ generated by $n$ projections summing to $\alpha\cdot 1$ admits a finite-dimensional representation."

**And three lines later, in the same paragraph:**
> "for $n\ge5$, $\Sigma_n$ contains the real interval $[\alpha_n,\beta_n]$ … [KRS02, Thm. 6] is the sharp statement: a finite-dimensional representation of $P_{n,\alpha}$ exists **iff** $\alpha$ is rational and $\alpha\in\Sigma_n$."

**Why it fails.** As defined, $\Sigma_n$ is a set of rationals, so it cannot contain a real interval; and the "iff" degenerates to "iff $\alpha\in\Sigma_n$", which is vacuous. The definition in the literature is the *Hilbert-space existence* set. Verified against primary text — Shulman, arXiv:0707.3053, p. 1: "the set $\Sigma_n$ of all scalars $\lambda$ such that the operators $\lambda 1$ are sums of $n$ projections… $\lambda\in\Sigma_n$ if and only if the relation (1) has a representation in a **separable Hilbert space**"; and the KRS02 abstract: "the set of all real numbers $\alpha$ admitting a collection of projections $P_1,\dots,P_n$ **on a Hilbert space $H$**." Everything downstream (the window, §6.2's "point of the discrete part of $\Sigma_5$", §2.1's $\Sigma_4$ accumulation) is stated for the Hilbert-space set, so this is a definitional slip, not a propagating error — but it is the first display of §2 and it is embarrassing.

**Repair (drop-in):**
> For $n\ge2$ let $\Sigma_n\subseteq[0,n]$ be the set of $\alpha$ for which $\alpha\cdot 1$ is a sum of $n$ orthogonal projections on **some Hilbert space**; equivalently (Shulman, p. 1), for which the universal $C^\ast$-algebra $P_{n,\alpha}$ is non-zero. Write $\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{fd}}\subseteq\Sigma_n$ for the subset realisable in a matrix algebra. […] [KRS02, Thm. 6] together with the trace obstruction gives $\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{fd}}=\Sigma_n\cap\mathbb Q$.

While repairing, note that Shulman also records $\Sigma_1=\{0,1\}$ and gives $\Sigma_4$ explicitly as $\{0,1,1+\frac{k}{k+2},2,3-\frac{k}{k+2},3,4\}$ — the paper's prose description ("infinite, discrete and rational, accumulating at $2$ from both sides") is correct and can cite it precisely.

## F2 — BLOCKING. "Flat to six significant figures from $N=17$ onwards" is false, and the paper's own table two lines below shows it is false.

**Quotes (three places — Abstract, Theorem 1.1(B), Theorem B):**
> Abstract: "flat to six figures over $3.76$ decades of dimension and $15.05$ decades of $\varepsilon$."
> Thm 1.1(B): "flat to six figures across $N = 17 \to 98\,209$ and $\varepsilon = 1.08\times10^{-5}\to 9.64\times10^{-21}$."
> Thm B: "the convergence is flat to six significant figures from $N=17$ onwards."

**Why it fails.** Against the asymptote $A=0.9732489895$, the engine's own table gives:

| $N$ | $N\varepsilon^{1/4}$ | relative deviation | significant figures agreeing |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 17 | 0.974133 | $9.1\times10^{-4}$ | **~3** |
| 72 | 0.973200 | $5.0\times10^{-5}$ | ~4.3 |
| 305 | 0.973252 | $3.1\times10^{-6}$ | ~5.5 |
| 1292 | 0.973249 | $1.1\times10^{-8}$ | ≥6 |

Six-figure flatness starts at $N=1292$, not $N=17$. The reader can see this by reading down the table's last column (`0.974133`, `0.973200`, `0.973252`) — the paper contradicts itself inside one page.

The two decade-spans inherit the error: $3.76$ decades and $15.05$ decades are $\log_{10}(98209/17)$ and $\log_{10}(1.0781{\times}10^{-5}/9.6447{\times}10^{-21})$, i.e. they are the spans of the *table*, not of the six-figure plateau. From $N=1292$ the honest spans are **1.88 decades of $N$ and 7.52 decades of $\varepsilon$**.

**The engine does not check the paper's claim.** `verify_dimension_law.py` C14 tests `abs(r - ASY) < 5e-3` over `N >= 17` — a tolerance three orders of magnitude looser than "six figures". C14 passes a statement the paper does not make.

(a) Keep the span, weaken the claim: "monotonically convergent across $N=17\to98\,209$ ($3.76$ decades of $N$, $15.05$ of $\varepsilon$), and flat to six significant figures from $N=1292$ ($1.88$ decades of $N$, $7.52$ of $\varepsilon$)." (b) Keep "six figures", move the start: "flat to six figures across $N=1292\to98\,209$". Tighten C14 to two assertions: `< 5e-3` for the `N>=17` tail and `< 5e-7` for the `N>=1292` tail, so the engine certifies the sentence actually printed.

## F3 — BLOCKING. "Tight to a factor $2.176$" compares a **denominator**-convention upper constant against an **actual-dimension** lower constant. The paper's own §5.1 table says the factor is not 2.176 in either convention.

**Quotes (§4.2, Remark 3.5, and the Abstract):**
> §4.2: "Against Theorem A's floor $0.4472$ the law is **$\Theta$, tight to a factor $2.176$** (check C15)."
> Rem. 3.5: "The residual factor $2.176$ between Theorem A's constant $0.4472$ and Theorem B's attained $0.9732$…"
> Abstract: "$N\varepsilon^{1/4}\to 0.9732489895$ along the convergents against an unconditional floor of $0.4472135955$."

**Why it fails.** §5.1 states explicitly that "every table in §4 is in the denominator convention", and Theorem A is stated for $N=\max_l n_l$, the *actual block dimension*. §5.1's own table then gives the two consistent comparisons:

* denominator convention: lower $\ge 0.2236$, upper $0.9732$ → **factor $4.35$**;
* actual dimension: lower $0.4472$, upper $4.866$–$9.732$ (Cor. 4.8) → **factor $10.9$–$21.8$**.

$2.176 = 0.9732/0.4472$ appears in neither row. Check C15 computes `ASY / FLOOR` and so faithfully reproduces the cross-convention number without noticing the mismatch. The Abstract's juxtaposition is the single most quotable sentence in the paper and it is convention-crossed.

**Repair — and it is a strict improvement, not a retreat.** Theorem A's own proof gives the *denominator* statement directly, and the paper is losing a factor 2 for nothing. In the proof, write $s_l=p_l/q_l$ in lowest terms (so $q_l\mid n_l$); then
$$|s_l-\sqrt5|=\frac{|p_l-q_l\sqrt5|}{q_l}\ \ge\ \frac{\lVert q_l\sqrt5\rVert}{q_l}\ >\ \frac{1}{5q_l^2}\ \ge\ \frac{1}{5Q^2},\qquad Q:=\max_l q_l,$$
so $\varepsilon>1/(25Q^4)$ with $Q\le N$ — i.e. Theorem A holds verbatim in the **denominator** convention, with the *same* constant $0.4472$, and implies the actual-dimension form. Then:

* replace §5.1's "$\ge 0.2236$ (Thm. A via $q\le d\le 2q$)" by "$0.4472$ (Thm. A, directly)" — this also removes the paper's only dependence of the lower bound on Lemma 4.6;
* say "**tight to a factor $2.176$ in the denominator convention**" in §4.2 and Rem. 3.5, and add "in actual dimension the corresponding gap is a factor $10.9$–$21.8$ (Cor. 4.8)";
* in the Abstract, name the convention in the same sentence.

Add a check: assert the Diophantine step at the reduced denominator $q_l$, not only at $n_l$.

## F5 — BLOCKING. §5.4's conclusion is wrong in the paper's own metric: $\varphi$ is *not* "twice as hard", and along its convergents $\varphi$ is strictly **cheaper** than $\sqrt5$.

**Quote (§5.4):**
> "The golden ratio $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$ lies in $W$ and is *twice as hard*: $\liminf_q q\lVert q\varphi\rVert = 1/\sqrt5$, $\liminf_q q\lVert q\sqrt5\rVert=1/\sqrt{20}$, ratio exactly $2$ (check C25). $\varphi$ is the extremal instance in the window, by Hurwitz; $\sqrt5$ is a factor $2$ cheaper."

**Why it fails.** The liminf ratio is indeed exactly 2 (C25 is correct). But the paper's own quantities are $D(\varepsilon)$ constants, and neither of them moves by a factor 2.

*Lower (floor).* With $c=\liminf q\lVert q\alpha\rVert$, Theorem A's chain gives $N\varepsilon^{1/4}>c^{1/2}$: $\varphi$ gives $5^{-1/4}=0.66874$, $\sqrt5$ gives $20^{-1/4}=0.47287$. **Ratio $\sqrt2$, not $2$.**

*Upper (attained).* Prop. 4.1 generalises verbatim, $\varepsilon=(\lambda_1-\alpha)(\alpha-\lambda_2)$, so along convergents
$$N\varepsilon^{1/4}\longrightarrow \Big(\frac{\lim k_{n+1}/k_n}{\alpha-\alpha'}\Big)^{1/2}.$$
For $\sqrt5$: $((2+\sqrt5)/(2\sqrt5))^{1/2}=0.9732490$. For $\varphi$: $(\varphi/\sqrt5)^{1/2}=\mathbf{0.8506508}$. Computed in 60-digit `Decimal` over 30 convergents of each, the tails are flat at `0.9732490` and `0.8506508` respectively. **$\varphi$ costs $\approx13\%$ *less* dimension than $\sqrt5$ at the same $\varepsilon$.** The badly-approximable-ness raises the *floor* but the slow convergent growth ($\varphi$ vs $2+\sqrt5$) lowers the *attained constant* by more.

So "$\sqrt5$ is a factor $2$ cheaper" is false in both directions, and "$\varphi$ is the extremal instance in the window" is only true for the Hurwitz liminf, which is not a $D(\varepsilon)$ statement. Since §5.4 exists precisely to say what "Diophantine rather than universal" means, getting the dependence wrong there is worse than omitting it.

**Repair.** Replace the paragraph with the honest two-parameter statement, which is *stronger* and is the natural next display:
> For a quadratic irrational $\alpha\in W$ with conjugate $\alpha'$ and convergent-denominator growth $\rho=\lim k_{n+1}/k_n$, the same two arguments give
> $$N\varepsilon^{1/4}\;>\;\big(\liminf_q q\lVert q\alpha\rVert\big)^{1/2}\quad\text{and}\quad N\varepsilon^{1/4}\longrightarrow\Big(\frac{\rho}{\alpha-\alpha'}\Big)^{1/2}.$$
> At $\alpha=\sqrt5$ these are $0.4729$ and $0.9732$; at $\alpha=\varphi$ they are $0.6687$ and $0.8506$. The floor is *higher* at $\varphi$ (Hurwitz: $\varphi$ is the worst-approximable point of $W$) while the attained constant is *lower*, because $\rho$ is smaller. The two ends of the law move in opposite directions with the arithmetic — which is the precise sense in which the law is Diophantine rather than universal.

Add a check: recompute both constants for $\varphi$ and $\sqrt5$ in extended precision and assert the four numbers.

## F6 — BLOCKING. §6.3's closing sentence is refuted by §6.4's own table, and the clause before it is false.

**Quote (§6.3, final paragraph):**
> "Moreover the argument is structural rather than a matter of two awkward examples: by [KRS02, Thm. 6] **every** rational in the window is attained, while orbit type is constant on $\Phi_5$-orbits and takes both values there. **No orbit-level predicate can separate attained from unattained parameters in the window.**"

**Why it fails, twice.**

1. *"takes both values there"* — in the window it does not. §2.3 proves (and check C4 confirms) $W^\circ=\{\mathfrak q<0\}$, so $\mathfrak q<0$ at **every** in-window parameter carrying an integral dimension vector. The section's own two witnesses are $\lambda=5/2$ (in the window, $\mathfrak q=-1$) and $\lambda=4/3$ (**outside** the window — §6.2 and C21 say so explicitly, $4/3<\lambda_-$, $\mathfrak q=+5$). Both values are taken on $\Sigma_5$, never inside $W$.

2. *"No orbit-level predicate can separate…"* — false as literally stated, and §6.4's own table says why. $\Phi_5$ is a Möbius map with integer coefficients and $\det=1$, so it maps $\mathbb Q$ bijectively to $\mathbb Q$ and irrationals to irrationals: **rationality is an orbit invariant.** §6.4 then states "attained? — invariant: rationality of $\lambda$". So "the orbit consists of rationals" *is* an orbit-level predicate that decides attainment in the window, exactly. Equivalently: "some rung carries an integral dimension vector" — which §6.1 itself uses to argue the $\sqrt5$ descent cannot terminate.

The result §6 actually proves — and it is a good one — is narrower: *no **topological or dynamical** invariant of the orbit (closure, termination, periodicity, multiplier, conjugacy class) decides attainment.* The Abstract already says exactly this correctly ("no orbit-closure or orbit-termination criterion can decide attainment here"); it is only §6.3 that overreaches.

**Repair.**
> …by [KRS02, Thm. 6] every rational in the window is attained, and $\Phi_5$ preserves $\mathbb Q$, so attainment *is* an orbit invariant — but an arithmetic one. What §6.2–§6.3 show is that **no topological or dynamical invariant of the orbit** — closure, termination under descent, periodicity, multiplier, $SL_2$-conjugacy class of the linearisation — decides it: $4/3$ and $\sqrt5$ agree on all of them and disagree on attainment, while $4/3$ and $5/2$ are both attained and disagree on descent termination.

## F8 — §1.1 misattributes DPP Thm. 4.2 to the wrong correlation set.

**Quote (§1.1):**
> "For $n=5$, DPP Thm. 4.2 shows $C_{qs}(5,2)$ is not closed, and Cor. 4.4 extends this to $C_q(5,2)$ and yields $C_{qs}(5,2)\neq C_{qa}(5,2)$."

## F9 — "The constant is pinned" is an overclaim, and §5.2 contradicts Remark 3.5 about which constant is "sharp".

**Quotes:**
> Abstract: "**The constant is pinned:** $N\varepsilon^{1/4}\to 0.9732489895$ along the convergents against an unconditional floor of $0.4472135955$."
> §5.2: "We report the subsequence constant as **the sharp one** and the uniform constant as the honest one."
> Rem. 3.5: "closing it would require knowing, for each $N$, the best pair of rationals with denominators $\le N$ straddling $\sqrt5$, which is the three-distance problem for $\sqrt5$."

**Why it fails.** $D(\varepsilon)$ is a *minimum over all carriers*. Theorem B is an upper bound from one two-parameter family; nothing rules out a better carrier (a non-convergent straddling pair, or a $\ge3$-block carrier) at any given $\varepsilon$. Remark 3.5 concedes exactly this. So the asymptotic constant of $D$ is *bracketed* in $[0.4472,\,0.9732]$ — a factor-$2.18$ window — not "pinned", and $0.9732$ is not "the sharp one", it is the sharpest *upper* constant currently exhibited. Calling it sharp while Remark 3.5 says the per-$N$ optimum is an open problem is an internal contradiction.

**Repair.** Abstract: "The constant is bracketed to a factor $2.18$: $N\varepsilon^{1/4}\to 0.9732489895$ along the convergents, against an unconditional floor of $0.4472135955$ (same convention — see F3)." §5.2: "the subsequence constant as the sharpest *attained* one".

## F10 — Remark 3.5's "everything else is an identity" is false.

**Quote (Rem. 3.5):**
> "The chain has one lossy step, the passage from $\lVert n_l\sqrt5\rVert>1/(5n_l)$ to the asymptotic $1/\sqrt{20}\,n_l$. **Everything else is an identity.**"

**Why it fails.** At least two further steps in Theorem A's chain are strict inequalities on any carrier the paper actually builds:

The remark's *conclusion* (the residual gap is genuine, not methodological) is defensible; the supporting sentence is not.

**Repair:** "The chain has one lossy step that is quantitatively controlled — the passage from $1/(5q)$ to $1/(\sqrt{20}\,q)$, worth a factor $(5/\sqrt{20})^{1/2}=1.057$ — plus two inequalities that are strict on the extremal family (the dropped within-block variance, and $n_l\le N$). Even with the tight Diophantine constant a factor $0.9732/0.4729=2.058$ remains, so the gap is genuine…"

## F11 — Lemma 4.5 and Lemma 4.6 are stated with universal quantifiers but supported by finite computation only.

**Quotes:**
> Lemma 4.5: "*For **every** $\alpha=m/l\in(3/2,2)$*… terminates after $s=m-l$ blocks…" followed by "*Proof sketch.*… Verified by re-executing the recursion in exact rational arithmetic for all $7\,921$ values $m/l\in(3/2,2)$ with $l\le179$."
> Lemma 4.6: "*Then for **every** $p/q$ in the window $q\le d_5(p/q)\le 2q$.*" — "Verified on $4\,000$ random in-window rationals."

**Why it fails.** The "proof sketch" of 4.5 does the dimension bookkeeping *conditional on* the recursion terminating with an admissible $k_j\in\{1,2,3\}$ at every step; that termination is exactly what is only checked to $l\le179$ (K1 confirms 7921/7921, but $l\le179$ is not "every"). Lemma 4.6 then quantifies over all in-window $p/q$ on a 4000-point sample (and see F4). Neither is a proof.

This is not fatal — the statements are almost certainly true, and K1–K3/T2 are strong evidence — but a referee will not accept "Lemma" for either as drafted, and the paper's own §8 sentence "Every numbered claim above is either a citation or a machine check" (F13) invites the challenge.

**Repair.** Either (a) prove termination: at each step $0<3-b_j-k_j\epsilon\le\epsilon$ with $b_{j+1}=\alpha-(3-b_j-k_j\epsilon)$ keeps $b_j\in[1,\alpha]$ and decreases $3l-m-\sum_{i\le j}k_i$ by $k_j\ge1$, so the recursion terminates in $\le 3l-m-1$ steps; write the two-line induction. Or (b) relabel both as **Proposition (verified computationally for $l\le179$ / on a 4000-point sample)** and state the general case as a conjecture. (a) is cheap and is the right move. T2's exhaustive lowest-terms/denominator bookkeeping for all $q<400$ is genuinely a proof of the transport step and should be presented as such.

## F24 — Engine hygiene: three checks certify less than their labels claim.

* **C1 is an algebraic tautology.** It asserts `s_claim/E == (n*t*t - t)/(n-1)` where `s_claim = n*t*(n*t-1)` and `E = n*n-n`; the two sides are identically equal for all $t$, $n$, by cancellation. Table 8.1 lists C1 as certifying "$f_{\mathrm{vect}}(t)=nt(nt-1)$ **is the active PSD constraint (DPP Prop. 4.1)**", which C1 does not test at all. (For the record, I verified DPP Prop. 4.1 directly: it is the three-branch piecewise function $0$ / $nt(nt-1)$ / $(n^2-n)(2t-1)$, and the paper's §1.1 quotes the middle branch with the correct interval $[1/n,(n-1)/n]$. The *paper* is right; the *check* is empty.) Either relabel C1 as an internal consistency check or implement the actual Cholesky/PSD derivation.
* **C8 contains dead code with a meaningless formula:** `val = F(qd * abs(five_q2 - m * m), 5 * qd)` (which is just `|5q²-m²|/5`, not $q\lVert q\sqrt5\rVert$) is accumulated into `worst`, which is then never used. Delete or fix.

(It is a classical fact; the label should say "consistent with", or the check should sweep.) Note also that $\inf_q q\lVert q\sqrt5\rVert < 1/\sqrt{20}$ — at $q=4$ it is $0.222912$ — so the "asymptotically" qualifier in §3.3 is doing real work and must not be dropped.
